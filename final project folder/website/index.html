The problem in the field

Warning systems see the river at a point. Nobody sees what that flow floods.

ClimMaTech, our industry partner, forecasts river level and flow 30 minutes to 48 hours ahead and warns villages through sirens and an app. Each of those numbers belongs to one gauge. Their own architecture lists a map-based River View layer for flood risk; this project builds what such a layer needs for one delta.

What exists

Radar gauges on bridges and spillways, solar sirens, a control room and the Aagah app, all working from the water level at a point.

What is missing

Which fields, villages, roads, bridges and embankments go under at the forecast flow, and who must be warned first.

Where

The Mahanadi delta below Naraj: 3,038 km² of study box, 4.1 million people, a damaging flood every few years.

Who uses it

The partner's control room, the Odisha Water Resources Department and CWC, and the district administrations that order evacuations.

Refined problem statement

For the Mahanadi delta, turn a river-flow forecast at Naraj into a map of what floods and whom it affects, with a satellite model chosen by a fair benchmark, and use that map to plan where warnings and sensors go, checked and calibrated at the site.

Remote sensing · water resources

A flood atlas of the delta, linked to the flow at Naraj.

Every Sentinel-1 pass since 2017, mapped through monsoon cloud and matched with the river.

The radar model mapped all 590 passes over the delta. Water outside the river bed and the dry-season water is flood: 1,137 km² went under at least once. Matching each pass with the GloFAS discharge at Naraj gives every 20 m pixel the flow at which it starts to flood, so a forecast flow becomes a map.

The river is not the only driver

The same river flow floods three times as much land in a week of heavy rain.

The largest floods in the record (September 2021 and August 2020) came with heavy rain, not with the highest river flows. Looking up past passes with a similar flow and similar 7-day rain shows the effect for the 5-year flow:

The learned flow + rain model gives a similar picture: at the 5-year flow it floods 30 km² in a dry week, 44 km² with typical rain and 142 km² with heavy rain.

Floodplain flooding is patchy and the observed maps are themselves radar maps, so pass-level scores are modest. Where the delta floods is very predictable from its past floods (ranking skill 0.90); how much of it floods on a given day only partly (0.72 for the trigger map). The frequency map says where to prepare, the trigger map at which flow to act, and the rain forecast has to be read with the river forecast.

Remote sensing · model selection

On a flood it had never seen, a U-Net beat every foundation model.

92 training runs on Sen1Floods11 with one recipe: six geospatial foundation models (Prithvi-EO-2.0, TerraMind, DOFA, Clay, Satlas, DeCUR), frozen and fine-tuned, against a CNN and a U-Net, three seeds each. The test that matters is the leave-India-out split: a 2016 Assam flood that no model saw in training.

Each run used the arguments it was queued with; no chip sits in two splits; under leave-India-out no India chip reaches training or validation; every test score recomputed from the saved predictions matches.

Matches the published TerraMind numbers

Our TerraMind-B on the official Sen1Floods11 test: mean IoU 89.7 with radar and optical, 79.8 with radar alone; the TerraMind paper (arXiv:2504.11171) reports 90.62 and 80.39.

Transportation systems

A flooded road is a lost hospital trip.

The OpenStreetMap road network becomes a graph, flooded sections are cut, and each village's trip to a hospital, a town and a dry school is compared with dry weather. In the 25-year flood 52,423 people in 41 settlements lose road access to every hospital, and everyday trips grow by about 10,077 person-hours a day. Ten critical links carry most of the cut-off routes; raising them is costed under construction.

Structural

Brick walls crack at about a metre of water; bridge piers must go deep.

Flood depth comes from the ground elevation at the flood edge. A 230 mm brick wall cracks at about 0.8–1.0 m of water, and 4,080 people live where the 25-year flood is a metre deep or more. At 11 major bridges, IRC:78 scour reaches 7–24 m below the high flood level if a branch carries a quarter of the 100-year flow; how the flow splits between branches is the missing input.

Geotechnical

River banks to inspect, and dry houses on soaked ground.

Bank cells are ranked by how often water stands behind the bank, the soil and the people nearby, giving twenty candidate sites; the top twelve are on the field team's GPS file for inspection and soil samples. Houses that stay dry but stand within 200 m of flood water on silty clay sit on soaked ground: 441,605 people in the 25-year flood. Soaking cuts a footing's bearing capacity by about 55 %, and the clay settles when the water table drops, the cracks that appear weeks after a flood.

Ocean engineering

The Bay of Bengal floods the delta from above.

37 cyclones and depressions passed within 300 km of the delta since 2016, and 11 of the 20 largest floods seen by radar followed one; 13 came with heavy rain. Surge and high tide also hold back the drainage of the lower delta, so a tide record is the first ocean dataset to add.

Construction technology and management

Build in December–April; raise the links that matter.

Twenty-nine years of river flow and 46 years of storm tracks leave December–April free of flood days and nearly free of storms, the window for road raising, embankment repair and installing sensors. Raising 10 critical links takes about 27,603 m³ of earthwork (about ₹0.97 crore at an assumed rate); none needs a raise above 2.1 m, so no bridge or vented causeway is needed.

Water resources and the watershed

Half of a flood peak comes from below the dam.

Hirakud dam controls about 59 % of the Mahanadi basin and passes 60% of the July–September flow at Naraj, but only about half of a yearly flood peak (median 48% over 29 years): the rest comes from the 58,000 km² of tributaries below the dam, within about a day. A delta warning needs the dam's release plan and gauges on those tributaries. A Gumbel fit to 29 years of annual maxima at Naraj gives 17,897 m³/s for the 2-year flood and 32,997 for the 25-year (11.7 lakh cusec).

Varshaq · rain gauge

Rain that is not yet linked to flooded land

The largest floods of the delta came with heavy rain, not with the highest river flows. Rain changes which land floods at the same flow. A learned flow + rain + history model, tested on years it never saw, maps heavy-rain passes better than the trigger map (IoU 0.22 against 0.15) and ranks flood-prone land best (AUC 0.95).

Prediction model · water resources

How far ahead the warning sees.

Water reaches Naraj from the Hirakud reach and the Tel and Ong tributaries in about a day. A gradient-boosted model per lead time reads today's flows at Naraj, Tikarpara and below Hirakud and the rain over the catchment between them, and forecasts the Naraj flow one to three days ahead.

Trained on 1997–2015 and tested on the monsoons of 2016–2026 it never saw, it keeps its skill where persistence fades: Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency 0.89 three days ahead against 0.79. A rain forecast adds most where it matters, in catching floods above the 2-year flow. With Pravaah-type gauges upstream, the same code trains on measured flows.

Deployment

From a forecast to a bulletin in seconds.

A forecast flow at Naraj (m³/s or lakh cusec, and optionally the forecast rain) goes in; a flood map, the settlements to warn and the roads under water come out, ready for a control room or a phone alert.
